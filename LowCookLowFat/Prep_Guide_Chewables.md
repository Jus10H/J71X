# Chewables Preparation Guide

## Overview

These foods are eaten alongside the shake, not blended into it. They provide crucial nutrients that benefit from chewing (jaw health, satiety) and require specific preparation for maximum nutrition.

## Optimal Cooking Methods

### Broccoli: "Hack and Hold" + Mustard Powder

**Why this matters:** Broccoli's star compound is **sulforaphane**, one of the most potent activators of the body's antioxidant defense system (Nrf2 pathway). Sulforaphane doesn't exist in intact broccoli — it's created when the enzyme **myrosinase** meets the precursor **glucoraphanin** after cell walls are broken. Heat destroys myrosinase, but sulforaphane itself is heat-stable once formed.

**Critical note on frozen broccoli:** Commercial frozen broccoli is blanched before freezing, which **destroys the myrosinase enzyme**. The "hack and hold" method will NOT work on frozen broccoli. You must use the mustard powder method.

**Method:**
1. Thaw frozen broccoli (microwave, room temp, or fridge overnight)
2. **Lightly steam for 1-3 minutes** — until bright green with slight crunch
3. **Sprinkle a pinch of mustard powder** on the broccoli before eating

The mustard powder provides its own myrosinase enzyme, which converts the (heat-stable) glucoraphanin into sulforaphane in your gut. As little as **half a teaspoon** is enough. Other myrosinase sources: daikon radish, horseradish, wasabi.

**Bonus:** Steaming nearly **doubles lutein levels** (important for eye and brain health) by disrupting cell walls and increasing bioavailability.

**Avoid:**
- **Boiling** — leaches water-soluble nutrients into the water
- **Microwaving** — detrimental for lutein retention specifically
- **High-heat stir-frying** — nearly eliminates lutein

> *Source: [NutritionFacts.org - How to Cook Broccoli](https://nutritionfacts.org/blog/how-to-cook-broccoli/), [Second Strategy to Cooking Broccoli](https://nutritionfacts.org/video/second-strategy-to-cooking-broccoli/)*

### Cauliflower: Steam + Mustard Powder

**Why this matters:** Cauliflower contains **glucosinolates** (the same family of anti-cancer compounds as broccoli) and significant **polyphenols** and **flavonoids**. The same myrosinase issue applies.

**Same frozen caveat:** Frozen cauliflower has no active myrosinase. Use mustard powder.

**Method:**
1. Thaw frozen cauliflower
2. **Steam for 2-3 minutes** — just until tender
3. **Sprinkle a pinch of mustard powder** before eating

**Why steaming wins:**
| Method | Glucosinolate Loss |
|--------|--------------------|
| Steaming | 19% |
| Blanching (boiling + cold water) | 42% |
| Boiling | 55% |

**Avoid:**
- **Boiling** — 55% glucosinolate loss, significant polyphenol/flavonoid loss into the water
- **Extended cooking beyond 3 minutes** — losses increase substantially with time

> *Source: [NutritionFacts.org - Best Way to Cook Vegetables](https://nutritionfacts.org/blog/what-is-the-best-way-to-cook-vegetables/), [PMC - Effect of Cooking on Cauliflower](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6615361/)*

### Mushrooms: Microwave 2-3 Minutes — NEVER Eat Raw

**Why this matters:** Mushrooms provide two unique benefits no other food in this protocol covers:

1. **Ergothioneine** — dubbed the "longevity vitamin" by biochemist Bruce Ames. Of 100+ compounds measured in the bloodstreams of thousands of people, ergothioneine was the single compound most associated with the lowest rates of heart disease, stroke, and death from all causes over 20+ years. Blood levels decline after age 60, correlating with cognitive decline and frailty. **Shiitake mushrooms have the highest ergothioneine content** of any mushroom tested. White button and chanterelle have negligible amounts.

2. **Aromatase inhibition** — In a landmark study comparing vegetables' ability to suppress an enzyme used by breast cancer cells, **plain white button mushrooms came in #1**, suppressing aromatase activity by more than 60% (vs ~20% for broccoli, carrots, spinach). Just 5 mushrooms per day may be sufficient. White button also boosted IgA antibody production by 50% after one week of consumption.

**Why you must cook them:** Raw mushrooms (especially button, cremini, portobello) contain **agaritine**, a potentially carcinogenic compound. Raw shiitake can cause **flagellate dermatitis** (a painful skin reaction). Cooking destroys agaritine and makes nutrients more bioavailable by breaking down chitin cell walls.

**Method:**
1. Slice shiitake (~1 oz) and white button (~5 mushrooms)
2. Place on a microwave-safe plate
3. **Microwave on high for 2-3 minutes**
4. Eat alongside the shake

**Why microwaving wins:**
| Method | Antioxidant Retention | Agaritine Reduction |
|--------|----------------------|---------------------|
| **Microwave** | **Best** | **~67% (reduced to 1/3)** |
| **Grilling** | **Best** | Good |
| Boiling | Significant loss (leaches into water) | Good (but agaritine leaches into broth) |
| Frying | Significant loss | 35-70% |

Agaritine reduction plateaus at ~67% regardless of cooking time — longer than 3 minutes won't reduce it further but will start degrading antioxidants.

**Avoid:**
- **Eating raw** — agaritine exposure + shiitake dermatitis risk
- **Boiling** — nutrients and agaritine both leach into water
- **Frying** — significant antioxidant loss

> *Source: [NutritionFacts.org - Mushrooms](https://nutritionfacts.org/topics/mushrooms/), [NutritionFacts.org - Ergothioneine](https://nutritionfacts.org/video/dietary-sources-of-the-longevity-vitamin-ergothioneine/), [NutritionFacts.org - Breast Cancer vs Mushrooms](https://nutritionfacts.org/video/breast-cancer-vs-mushrooms/), [NutritionFacts.org - Are Raw Mushrooms Safe?](https://nutritionfacts.org/blog/are-raw-mushrooms-safe-to-eat/), [Schulzová et al. 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12396396/)*

### Sweet Potato: Boil Whole with Skin On

**Why this matters:** Orange sweet potato contains extraordinary amounts of **beta-carotene** (774% DV per cup when cooked). The cooking method makes a massive difference in how much you actually retain.

**Method:**
1. Scrub sweet potatoes but **leave the skin on**
2. Place whole in a pot, cover with water
3. Put a **tight lid** on the pot
4. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer
5. **Boil for ~20 minutes** or until fork-tender
6. Remove, let cool, then **refrigerate**
7. For the shake: scoop cold flesh from the skin into the blender

**Why boiling wins (Dr. Greger's recommendation):**
| Method | Vitamin A Retention | Peel Antioxidants | Glycemic Index |
|--------|--------------------|--------------------|----------------|
| **Boiling (lid on)** | **92%** | Preserved | **Low (~46)** |
| Microwaving | Moderate | Mostly preserved | Moderate |
| **Baking** | **~20% (80% loss)** | **2/3 destroyed** | **High (~94)** |

- The peel contains **nearly 10x the antioxidant power** of the flesh (comparable to blueberries). Baking destroys over two-thirds of it.
- Boiled sweet potatoes have roughly **half the glycemic index** of baked — less blood sugar spike.
- Cooling after cooking creates **resistant starch**, a prebiotic that feeds beneficial gut bacteria.

**Avoid:**
- **Baking/roasting** — 80% vitamin A loss, destroys peel antioxidants, doubles glycemic index
- **Deep frying** — generates acrylamide (potential carcinogen)
- **Cutting into small pieces before boiling** — more surface area = more nutrient leaching

> *Source: [NutritionFacts.org - Best Way to Cook Sweet Potatoes](https://nutritionfacts.org/blog/is-it-better-to-bake-boil-or-steam-sweet-potatoes/)*

### Onion: Raw When Possible, Baked When Cooking

**Why this matters:** Onions are rich in **quercetin**, a powerful flavonoid with senolytic (anti-aging), anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties. They also have **antiplatelet (blood-thinning) activity** — but these two benefits respond very differently to heat.

**The unique thing about onions:** They are one of only three vegetables (with artichokes and beets) whose **overall antioxidant capacity is NOT reduced by any cooking method** — even boiling.

**However, specific compounds are affected:**

| Compound | Best Method | Worst Method |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| Quercetin bioaccessibility | **Baking** (65-80% bioaccessible) | Boiling (76% quercetin lost to water) |
| Antiplatelet activity | **Raw** (abolished within 10 min of cooking) | Any cooking |
| Overall antioxidant capacity | Any method (unaffected) | None — always preserved |

**Recommended approaches:**
- **Best:** Eat raw — slice thin and eat alongside the shake as a chewable. Maximum quercetin + antiplatelet benefits.
- **Good:** Bake quartered — highest quercetin bioaccessibility of any cooking method.
- **Acceptable:** Dry saute 3-5 minutes in a hot pan without oil.

**Avoid:**
- **Boiling** — 76% quercetin loss (84.7% anthocyanin loss in red onions) because compounds leach into water
- **Do NOT boil with sweet potatoes** unless you drink the broth

> *Source: [NutritionFacts.org - Best Cooking Method](https://nutritionfacts.org/video/best-cooking-method/), [NutritionFacts.org - Raw vs Cooked Onions](https://nutritionfacts.org/blog/raw-vs-cooked-garlic-and-onions-for-blood-thinning/), [PMC - Onion Phenolic Bioaccessibility](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8151956/)*

## Weekly Batch Prep

### Sunday Prep (~30 minutes)

**Sweet Potatoes (batch for the week):**
1. Scrub 7 sweet potatoes, leave skin on
2. Place whole in a large pot, cover with water, lid on
3. Boil ~20 minutes until fork-tender
4. Cool on the counter, then refrigerate whole
5. Each morning, scoop cold flesh into blender

**Broccoli & Cauliflower:**
- Keep frozen until ready to use each day
- Each day: thaw a portion, steam 1-3 minutes, add mustard powder

**Mushrooms:**
- Store fresh shiitake and white button in the fridge in a paper bag (not plastic — they need airflow)
- Use within 5-7 days of purchase
- Each day: slice 1 oz shiitake + 5 white button, microwave 2-3 minutes

**Carrots & Bell Peppers:**
- Wash and store whole in the fridge
- Eat raw each day — no prep needed beyond rinsing

**Onion (if using):**
- Keep whole until ready to use
- Slice fresh each day for maximum benefit (raw)

## Quick Reference: Mustard Powder

Keep a small jar of regular yellow mustard powder next to where you eat. A pinch on broccoli and cauliflower is all it takes. This one simple habit can be the difference between getting sulforaphane or not.
